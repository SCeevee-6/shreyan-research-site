This note records Theorem A of the draft manuscript and the
data behind it. The graph is not new; what the manuscript adds is the explicit, independently
verified orbital algebra of the group action.<Note>The collinearity graph of the Ree–Tits octagon (Tits 1983); its description as a coset graph with automorphism group $T{:}2$ is in Crnković–Rukavina–Švob, Theorem 9 and Table 13.</Note>

## The action

The Tits group $T={}^2F_4(2)'$ has order $17\,971\,200=2^{11}\cdot3^3\cdot5^2\cdot13$. It has a
subgroup $H\cong 2.2^8{:}5{:}4$ of order $10\,240$, hence acts transitively on $\Omega=T/H$ with
$|\Omega|=1755$. The permutation representation of degree $1755$ is listed in the ATLAS of Group
Representations and has rank $5$ with subdegrees $1,10,80,640,1024$. Let $R_0,\dots,R_4$ be the
orbitals (with $R_0$ the diagonal) and $A_i$ their adjacency matrices; then
$A_iA_j=\sum_{k}p_{ij}^kA_k$ with non-negative integers $p_{ij}^k$.

<Theorem n="A" name="orbital algebra">
All five orbitals are symmetric, so the orbital algebra is a commutative $5$-class association
scheme, and $R_i$ is the distance-$i$ relation of the distance-regular graph $\Gamma=(\Omega,R_1)$
with intersection array $\{10,8,8,8;1,1,1,5\}$. The intersection matrices $B_i=(p_{ij}^k)_{j,k}$ are
given in (1), the eigenmatrix and multiplicities in (2).
</Theorem>

## How it was computed

Take $d=4$, $(s,t)=(2,4)$, so $m=\sqrt{2st}=4$. Then
$|\Omega|=(1+s)(1+st)(1+s^2t^2)=3\cdot9\cdot65=1755$, the valencies are $k_i=1,10,80,640,1024$, and
the array is $\{10,8,8,8;1,1,1,5\}$.

The orbitals were computed from the ATLAS generators of the permutation representation of degree
$1755$: the point stabilizer has orbits of lengths $1,10,80,640,1024$, a breadth-first search in the
graph of the valency-$10$ orbital produces the layers $1,10,80,640,1024$ from a base point, and the
$125$ intersection numbers $p_{ij}^k$ were obtained by counting, with a transporter element,
$|R_i(x_0)\cap R_j(y)|$ for a representative $y\in R_k(x_0)$. These $125$ numbers agree in every
entry with those derived from the intersection array by the recursion
$A_1A_i=b_{i-1}A_{i-1}+a_iA_i+c_{i+1}A_{i+1}$. The distance relations of $\Gamma$ are $T$-invariant
and there are five of them, equal to the rank, so each is a single orbital; in particular every
orbital is symmetric and the algebra is commutative.

## Intersection matrices

With $B_0=I$, and rows $j$, columns $k$:

$$
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 10 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 8 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 8 & 5\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
B_2=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 80 & 8 & 6 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 64 & 8 & 38 & 25 \\ 0 & 0 & 64 & 40 & 50\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
B_3=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 8 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 64 & 8 & 38 & 25 \\ 640 & 64 & 304 & 200 & 250 \\ 0 & 512 & 320 & 400 & 360\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
B_4=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 8 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 64 & 40 & 50 \\ 0 & 512 & 320 & 400 & 360 \\ 1024 & 512 & 640 & 576 & 608\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1}
$$

They satisfy $p_{ij}^k=p_{ji}^k$ and $\sum_kp_{ij}^kk_k=k_ik_j$.

## Eigenmatrix and multiplicities

The octagon eigenmatrix at $(s,t)=(2,4)$, $m=4$, gives

$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}1 & 10 & 80 & 640 & 1024 \\ 1 & 5 & 10 & 0 & -16 \\ 1 & 1 & -10 & -8 & 16 \\ 1 & -3 & 2 & 16 & -16 \\ 1 & -5 & 20 & -80 & 64\end{pmatrix}, \tag{2}
$$

and multiplicities

$$
(m_0,\dots,m_4)=(1,351,650,675,78),
$$

with eigenvalues $10,5,1,-3,-5$ of $A_1$ and $m_e=|\Omega|\big/\sum_jP_{ej}^2/k_j$.

## Krein parameters, and what the scheme is not

With $Q$ determined by $QP=|\Omega|I$, all Krein parameters $q_{ef}^g$ are non-negative and the only
vanishing one among the non-principal indices is $q_{44}^{4}=0$, where index $4$ is the eigenspace of
dimension $78$ (eigenvalue $-5$). This is an instance of the extremal-polygon phenomenon of
Cameron–Goethals–Seidel.

The scheme is not $Q$-polynomial with respect to any ordering of its eigenspaces. This is known: by
De Bruyn–Vanhove, for $d\ge4$ the only thick $Q$-polynomial regular near $2d$-gons are the dual polar
spaces and the Hamming near polygons, and a thick generalized octagon is neither. It was also checked
directly in exact arithmetic over all $24$ orderings.

The graph is not tight in the sense of Jurišić–Koolen–Terwilliger: with $k=10$, $a_1=1$, $b_1=8$,
$\theta_1=5$, $\theta_4=-5$ the fundamental bound

$$
\Bigl(\theta_1+\tfrac{k}{a_1+1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\theta_4+\tfrac{k}{a_1+1}\Bigr)\ge-\tfrac{ka_1b_1}{(a_1+1)^2}
$$

reads $0\ge-20$, which is strict; also $a_4=5\ne0$, while tight graphs have $a_d=0$.

Of the $15$ partitions of $\{1,2,3,4\}$, only the two trivial ones close, so the scheme has no proper
fusion. That is Corollary C of [Note 1](/mathematics/fusion-theorem).

## Checks on the group side

The order $|T|=17971200$ was re-derived from the generators by Schreier–Sims. The five eigenspace
characters were evaluated exactly on all $22$ classes and agree with irreducible characters of
degrees $1,78,351,650,675$ in the GAP character table library. The permutation character $1_H^T$ is
their sum,

$$
1_H^T = 1 + 78 + 351 + 650 + 675,
$$

so it is multiplicity-free, with $\langle\pi,\pi\rangle=5$. A character table computed from scratch
from the generators by Dixon–Schneider is equivalent to the library table. Uniqueness of
$\mathrm{GO}(2,4)$ as a generalized octagon is not addressed.
