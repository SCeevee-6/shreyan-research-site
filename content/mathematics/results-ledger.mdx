Most of what I computed for the [generalized-polygon project](/research/generalized-polygon-schemes)
didn't turn into a theorem. This is everything I tried and what happened, labelled **known** (in the literature), **new** (not found in the sources checked —
the check is a search, not a full review), or **negative** (the answer is that nothing exists).<Note>Each entry summarises one line of work: what was asked, how it was checked, and what came of it.</Note>

## Fusions of the polygon schemes

**Hexagons with $s=t$.** The fusion $\{A_0,\ A_1+A_2,\ A_3\}$ is the familiar embedding of the split
Cayley hexagon $H(q)$ in $Q(6,q)$. *Known.* The classification for all integer $s,t\ge 2$ is
Theorem B of [Note 1](/mathematics/fusion-theorem).

**Octagons.** The case $s=t$ is excluded by the Feit–Higman condition ($2st$ must be a square), so the
Ree–Tits octagon $\mathrm{GO}(2,4)$ has no proper fusion. The octagon statement was not found in the
sources checked. *New as a statement; it reduces to Feit–Higman.*

**Thin octagons.** Orders $(1,t)$ for $t=2,8,18,32,50$ and $(s,1)$ for $s=2,8$. For $(1,t)$ the only
proper fusions are the bipartition $\{1,3\}\,|\,\{2,4\}$ and the halved fusion
$\{2\}\,|\,\{1,3\}\,|\,\{4\}$, whose class 2 is two copies of the $\mathrm{GQ}(t,t)$ collinearity
graph. Order $(2,1)$ has the primitive fusion $\{3\}\,|\,\{1,2,4\}$, which is $\mathrm{SRG}(45,16,8,4)$,
the parameters of $T(10)$; whether the graph is isomorphic to $T(10)$ was not checked. Order $(8,1)$
has none. None is $Q$-polynomial. *Nothing new.*

**Regular near hexagons and octagons.** Diameter 3 is BCN Proposition 4.2.17. *Known.* For near
octagons a box scan ($s\le 8$, $t\le 24$) finds closing thick fusions only for generalized octagons
with $s=t$ (excluded by Feit–Higman), Hamming graphs and the $B_4/C_4$ dual polar graphs, all known.
*No general theorem.*

## The 1755-point scheme

**$Q$-polynomiality.** The point scheme of a thick generalized octagon is not $Q$-polynomial in any
ordering. *Known* (De Bruyn–Vanhove, 2015); the project's exact check reproduces it.

**Tightness.** The graph is not tight: the Jurišić–Koolen–Terwilliger bound reads $0\ge-20$, which is
strict. *Check.*

**Triple intersection numbers of $\mathrm{GO}(2,4)$.** With $q^4_{44}=0$, the LP ranges of the triple
intersection numbers reproduce octagon geometry for only one triple type. *Nothing new for
uniqueness.*

## Graphs with a transitive action of the Tits group

**Strongly regular and distance-regular graphs.** An exhaustive search over all unions of orbitals for
every action of $T$ of degree $7020$ to $17550$, including the primitive actions on $12480$ and
$14976$ points, found no strongly regular graph and no distance-regular graph of diameter at least 3
on $12480$ or $14976$ points; with Crnković–Rukavina–Švob (2020) the primitive case is complete. On
the imprimitive actions the only strongly regular graphs are complete multipartite, coming from the
block systems. Up to $17550$ vertices the only non-trivial strongly regular and distance-regular
graphs of diameter at least 3 with a transitive $T$-action are the four found by
Crnković–Rukavina–Švob. *New as a search range; negative — no new graph.*

**Fusions of the non-commutative configurations.** On $12480$ points there is no proper fusion of
rank at least 3; on $14976$ points every fusion of rank at least 3 refines the rank-16 orbital
configuration of ${}^2F_4(2)$ and is non-commutative. *New as far as found, and small.* Details in
[Note 3](/mathematics/tits-orbital-algebras).

## Beyond the Tits group

**Open SRG parameter sets and primitive groups.** All $2164$ primitive groups of rank at least 4 on
the $430$ open degrees $v\le 1300$ in Brouwer's table were searched. No open parameter set with
$v\le 1300$ is realised by a strongly regular graph with a primitive
automorphism group. The result is exact for $2150$ groups; for $14$ groups at least one parameter set
was settled only by a floating-point MILP, so for those it is numerical. *New (not found in the
sources checked); negative.*

**Forced fusions in Brouwer's distance-regular graph tables.** All closed fusions of all tabled arrays
of diameter at most 7 were computed, and fused strongly regular graphs on at most $1300$ vertices were
looked up in Brouwer's SRG table. Of the six arrays that looked open, $\{14,10,3;1,5,12\}$ and
$\{21,16,10,1;1,2,16,21\}$ fail the absolute bound themselves, $\{19,12,5;1,4,15\}$ and
$\{112,77,16;1,16,88\}$ are deleted in the BCN additions, and $\{39,30,4;1,5,36\}$ and
$\{25,24,2,1;1,2,24,25\}$ follow directly from a recent preprint of Kovaľ (conditional). *Known;
dropped as a paper.*

## Still open

Uniqueness of $\mathrm{GO}(2,4)$ as a generalized octagon is not addressed anywhere in the project. The
draft manuscript has not yet been compiled after its last preamble change, and its Section 3 is to be
rewritten around the exact Gröbner-basis proof.
