## Question

The distance relations of a distance-regular graph form an association scheme. A *fusion* merges
some of those relations so that the merged set is still closed under multiplication, giving a
smaller scheme — and, when only two classes remain, a strongly regular graph. The question is when
the point graph of a generalized $2d$-gon of order $(s,t)$ admits a proper fusion, for the hexagon
($d=3$) and octagon ($d=4$) cases.

## Motivation

Generalized polygons are among the most rigid objects in finite geometry, and the Tits group
${}^2F_4(2)'$ acts on the Ree–Tits octagon of order $(2,4)$ with five orbitals on $1755$ points.
Asking which unions of those orbitals form a scheme — in particular, whether any strongly regular
graph is a union of orbitals — leads directly to the general fusion question for the whole family of
intersection arrays.

## Methods

The argument works from the intersection array alone, so it applies to every distance-regular graph
with the array of a generalized polygon, not only to polygons that exist. A fusion criterion in terms
of the eigenmatrix $P$ reduces closure to the vanishing of differences of eigenvalue sums; a sign
analysis of those differences, together with divisibility arguments for integer $s,t\ge 2$, decides
every case. The result is backed by two independent exact computations: a direct closure test of every
proper partition for all $841$ integer pairs $2\le s,t\le 30$, and a certificate that writes the
closure conditions as polynomial equations in $\mathbb{Q}[s,t]$, reduces them with a lexicographic
Gröbner basis over $\mathbb{Q}$, and isolates the real solutions exactly.

## Result

<Theorem n={1} name="fusions of generalized hexagons and octagons">
Let $d\in\{3,4\}$ and let $\Gamma$ be a distance-regular graph with the intersection array of a
generalized $2d$-gon of order $(s,t)$, where $s,t\ge 2$ are integers. The distance scheme
$\{A_0,\dots,A_d\}$ has a proper fusion scheme if and only if $s=t$. In that case the fusion is
unique, namely $\{A_0,\ A_1+\dots+A_{d-1},\ A_d\}$, and it is strongly regular.
</Theorem>

For hexagons with $s=t=q$ the fusion has the parameters of the collinearity graph of $Q(6,q)$:
$$
\begin{gathered}\Bigl(\tfrac{q^6-1}{q-1},\ q(q+1)(q^2+1),\\
q^3+q^2+q-1,\ (q+1)(q^2+1)\Bigr).\end{gathered}
$$
For octagons, the Feit–Higman theorem requires $2st$ to be a perfect square, so $s=t$ never occurs
for a thick generalized octagon. Every thick finite generalized octagon therefore has a point scheme
with no proper fusion; in particular no strongly regular graph is a union of orbitals of the Tits
group on $1755$ points. The full statement and proof are in [§4, Note 1](/mathematics/fusion-theorem).

## Limitations

The manuscript is a draft. The hexagon case with $s=t$ is the
familiar embedding of the split Cayley hexagon $H(q)$ in $Q(6,q)$; the octagon case reduces, by the
corollary above, to Feit–Higman. A literature search did not find Theorem 1 stated in Brouwer–Cohen–Neumaier
or Crnković–Rukavina–Švob and the papers citing it, but the search was not exhaustive and the result
may be known to specialists. The hypothesis $s,t\ge 2$ is necessary: thin octagon schemes of orders
$(1,2)$, $(1,8)$ and $(2,1)$ do admit fusions. Uniqueness of the octagon $\mathrm{GO}(2,4)$ is not
addressed.

## Status

Proved, in a draft manuscript dated 28 October 2026 (*Fusions of the point–distance schemes of
generalized hexagons and octagons, and the orbital algebra of the Tits group on 1755 points*). The
companion investigation of other actions of the Tits group is a separate, ongoing project:
[Orbital configurations of the Tits group](/research/tits-group-orbital-configurations).
