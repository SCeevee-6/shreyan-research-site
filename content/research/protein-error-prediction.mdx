## Question

Protein language models like ESM-2 can score any amino-acid substitution without experimental
labels, from masked-token probabilities. Benchmarks such as ProteinGym grade them by the Spearman
correlation between predicted and measured effects across a whole assay. But if you have to decide
which individual predictions to act on, a whole-assay number doesn't help. You want to know, for
each mutation, how wrong the prediction is likely to be.

So the question was: can a model trained on some ProteinGym assays predict the per-mutation errors of
ESM-2 650M on proteins it has never seen, well enough to set the worst predictions aside — and better
than the model's own confidence signals, like the wild-type log-probability or the entropy of the
masked marginal?

## Methods

A gradient-boosted model of each mutation's absolute rank error was trained on $162$ ProteinGym
development assays, using information available at prediction time. It was evaluated once, under an
internally prespecified plan, on $49$ test assays held out by $30\%$ sequence-identity clustering.
The design was written down and hashed before any modelling, later changes were logged as dated
amendments, and only one confirmatory evaluation on the held-out set was allowed. That plan was kept in
my own records, not in an external registry.

The score is the normalized area under the risk–coverage curve: rank the predictions by how risky
they look, withhold the riskiest, and track the error of what's left as you keep fewer and fewer.

## Results

- **Against the baseline.** The error model's normalized area under the risk–coverage curve was
  $0.258$, against $0.067$ for the strongest baseline, the wild-type log-probability: a mean paired
  difference of $0.191$ $[0.143, 0.240]$, better in $44$ of $49$ assays.

- **A table practitioners can use.** A cross-assay table of substitution-pair errors, combined with
  the rank floor, recovered $0.80$ of the test-set gain over the baseline. The table is deposited
  with the code and can be used directly.
- **Proteins absent from training.** In a preregistered test on $13$ proteins absent from training,
  the error model again beat the baseline ($+0.095$ $[+0.010, +0.178]$), and did so on a
  floor-robust metric computed within rank deciles ($+0.057$ $[+0.005, +0.112]$), but not on its
  whole-assay version ($+0.002$ $[-0.094, +0.102]$). Its small increment over the pair table, real
  within ProteinGym, did not clearly transfer. A smaller preregistered test on new studies of the
  test proteins was inconclusive.

## Limitations

The prespecified negative control failed, and opening the test set was my decision. One
study shared between training and test inflates the headline effect. The model did not improve
top-50 selection, and it orders predictions rather than giving calibrated uncertainty.
