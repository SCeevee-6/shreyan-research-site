## Question

A transitive permutation group determines a coherent configuration: its orbitals on ordered pairs.
When that configuration is commutative it is an association scheme, and its fusions — unions of
orbitals closed under multiplication — include every strongly regular or distance-regular graph that
the group acts on transitively. For the Tits group $T={}^2F_4(2)'$, of order
$17\,971\,200 = 2^{11}\cdot 3^3\cdot 5^2\cdot 13$, the project computes these configurations for
the actions on cosets of its eight maximal subgroups and asks which fusions occur.

## Motivation

The rank-5 action on $1755$ points is the Ree–Tits octagon of order $(2,4)$, and the fusion question
there is settled by the classification in [the companion project](/research/generalized-polygon-schemes).
The other actions are not governed by a polygon, so their fusions have to be found from the
configurations themselves — including the non-commutative ones, where the theory of association
schemes does not apply directly.

## Methods

Orbitals are computed exactly from the ATLAS permutation generators: stabiliser orbits from Schreier
generators, integer intersection numbers, centre dimensions, eigenmatrices, tests of the $P$- and
$Q$-polynomial properties, and exhaustive tests of every partition of the orbitals for closure. Group-
theoretic data are cross-checked in GAP against the character table library; the order of $T$ is
re-derived by Schreier–Sims.

## Results so far

- **Commutativity.** On cosets of the maximal subgroups, the orbital configurations are commutative
  for degrees $1600$ (two classes), $1755$, $2304$ and $2925$; all of these are symmetric except
  degree $2304$. They are non-commutative for degree $12480$ (rank $24$, two classes) and degree
  $14976$ (rank $25$).
- **Non-commutative algebras.** The orbital algebras decompose as
  $11\,\mathbb{C}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})$ (degree $12480$) and
  $9\,\mathbb{C}\oplus 4\,M_2(\mathbb{C})$ (degree $14976$), with centre of dimension $13$ in both
  cases, computed exactly from the structure constants and in agreement with the character
  multiplicities.
- **Fusions of the commutative actions.** Degree $1600$: one proper fusion, the known
  $\mathrm{SRG}(1600,351,94,72)$. Degree $2304$: three fusions, none $P$- or $Q$-polynomial.
  Degree $2925$: one fusion, the distance scheme of the dual octagon $\mathrm{GO}(4,2)$, which is
  $P$- but not $Q$-polynomial. Degree $1755$: none, by the fusion theorem.
- **Fusions of the non-commutative actions.** The $12480$-point configurations have no proper fusion
  of rank at least $3$. Every fusion of rank at least $3$ of the $14976$-point configuration refines
  a rank-$16$ orbital configuration of ${}^2F_4(2)$ and is non-commutative.
- **Search for strongly regular and distance-regular graphs.** An exhaustive search over all unions
  of orbitals, for the actions of $T$ of degree $7020$ to $17550$, found no strongly regular graph
  and no distance-regular graph of diameter at least $3$ on $12480$ or $14976$ points; together with
  Crnković–Rukavina–Švob this completes the primitive case. On $7020$, $8775$, $11700$ and all
  three $17550$-point actions, only complete multipartite strongly regular graphs occur, coming from
  the block systems, and there is no distance-regular graph of diameter at least $3$. Up to $17550$
  vertices the only non-trivial strongly regular and distance-regular graphs with a transitive action
  of $T$ are the four found by Crnković–Rukavina–Švob. The search range is new; it produced no new
  graph.

Details are in [§4, Note 2](/mathematics/tits-1755) and [§4, Note 3](/mathematics/tits-orbital-algebras);
the negative results are collected in [§4, Note 4](/mathematics/results-ledger).

## Limitations

These are exact computations, not yet a written classification. The classification of fusion
lattices across all actions of $T$ is a research direction and is not complete. Several results recover known
objects; the non-commutative fusion results are new as far as a literature search found, and small.

## Status

Ongoing computational investigation.
