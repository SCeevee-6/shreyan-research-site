---
title: Dress-Up
kind: prose
form: Vignette
order: 4
---

Every morning, I wear new clothes. I stand in front of my closet for a few minutes, looking at everything I own, trying to decide what would look best today. Sometimes I already know. Other times, I try on three or four things before settling on something. My mother says I take too long getting ready. I tell her I am almost done.

On Mondays, I wear black. My parents like it when I look presentable, so I make sure my shirt is tucked in and my hair is combed. My collar is always a little too stiff, and I spend breakfast pulling at it. I try not to say too much. There are certain things I can say, and certain things I cannot. I have learned which ones belong at the breakfast table.

On Tuesdays, I wear something brighter. A yellow shirt, perhaps. Something that catches the light when I walk into a room. My friends like jokes, so I bring a few with me. I know when to laugh, when to talk, and when to pretend I understand something. Sometimes they talk about things I don't know much about. I nod anyway. I have never been particularly good at being the right amount of someone. Too quiet for one table, too loud for another. I spend a lot of time wondering if I should have worn something different.

Wednesdays are harder. There are people who like me for being smart, so I make sure I have something clever to say. There are people who like me for being funny, so I try to make them laugh. There are people who don't seem to like either, so I try something else. I change my shirt, fix my hair. I try to be louder, then quieter, then someone who doesn't care at all, and by the end of the day I have changed outfits five times.

Thursdays are usually spent fixing whatever I got wrong on Wednesday. I replay conversations in my head, remembering the clothes I wore and the clothes I should have worn instead. I wonder if someone noticed that I was trying too hard. I wonder if they noticed that I wasn't trying hard enough. I wish people came with dress codes. It would make getting ready much easier.

By Friday, I am tired. I come home, take off my shoes, and leave my bag by the door. My parents ask how my day was. I tell them it was good. My friends send messages. I answer some of them. Eventually, everyone goes to sleep, and I am left alone in my room.

My closet is still open.

I look through the clothes hanging inside. There is the shirt my parents like, the one my friends think looks good, and the one I wear when I want people to think I am doing fine. There are clothes for people I haven't seen in weeks, and clothes for people I see every day. Some of them don't even fit anymore, but I keep them anyway.

I take one off the hanger, then put it back. I try another. Then another. The hangers scrape against each other.

Nobody is home to tell me what looks good. Nobody is waiting for me to say something funny. Nobody needs me to be particularly smart, or particularly quiet, or particularly anything. I should be happy about that. Instead, I stand there for a while, holding a shirt I don't remember buying.

I close the closet.

Tomorrow is Saturday. I don't know what I will wear.
