---
title: ore
kind: poem
order: 4
---

There is both a forge and a river,
and my tongue serves them both.

The forge, alas, glows first.
 The bellows of anger pump air 
until every thought within turns red. 

Here, my tongue becomes a blacksmith’s hand, 
pounding words against my teeth 
until they gleam with a sharp edge. 

It is so simple to make blades; 
all it takes is heat and hurry. 

I can shape always and never, 
pound them thin, 
and slide them into weapons 
that fly straight and cut clean.

Blades have a powerful feeling when they leave you. 
It tastes like triumph. 
But you remember where it impales.

Later, once the forge is dead, 
I still hear the echo of the blade meeting bone, 
and see the glint of my own reflection
on the edge of my weapon.

That is when the river speaks. 

The river is older than the forge, 
patient as stone. 

It flows through the same chest that held the fire,
cooling the places that the forge scorched. 

Here, my tongue is not the blade, 
but a hand held open. 

Words flow into my mouth 
like smooth stones, tested by water 
before they ever touch the air.

A bridge begins as a single plank.
 It does not shine like the blade. 
When you lay it down, no one gasps.
 Yet, plank by plank, it will grow strong enough 
to carry both our names without breaking.

The world will never stop giving me ore.
 But the choice is mine, every time the fire flares: 
Shall I lift a knife, 
bright and lonely? 

Or lay it down, stone after stone, 
until there is a bridge where the wound used to be, 
and both of us 
may walk across?
